Dr. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar

1891 – 1956  ·  Architect of the Indian Constitution

Born into a community denied basic dignity, he became one of the most formally educated Indians of his generation — degrees from Bombay, Columbia University, and the London School of Economics — and turned that learning into a lifelong argument for equality.

As Chairman of the Drafting Committee, he shaped the Constitution of India, embedding fundamental rights, safeguards against discrimination, and a framework for social justice into the nation's founding law.

Beyond the Constitution, he was an economist, jurist, editor, and reformer — founding journals, building movements against untouchability, and arguing for labour rights, education, and the political voice of the marginalised.

  • Born 14 April 1891, Mhow, Central Provinces
  • Education Columbia University, LSE, Gray's Inn
  • Role Chairman, Constitution Drafting Committee
  • Legacy Bharat Ratna, 1990

A LIFE, IN RECORD

Milestones the archive is built around

1907 – 1923

Formal education abroad

Degrees from Columbia University and the London School of Economics, at a time few Indians of any background reached either.

1927

Mahad Satyagraha

Leads a march to assert the right of the depressed classes to draw water from a public tank — a founding act of the civil rights movement he led.

1947 – 1949

Drafting the Constitution

As Chairman of the Drafting Committee, shapes the document that defines India's fundamental rights and structure of government.

1956

Conversion at Nagpur

Leads a mass conversion to Buddhism, a final public act rooted in decades of argument against caste-based exclusion.

1990

Bharat Ratna

Posthumously awarded India's highest civilian honour, decades after his death in 1956.

Why did he call it "Annihilation of Caste" rather than reform?
Because he argued caste could not be reformed piece by piece — it had to be rejected as a system entirely, since its logic depended on hierarchy itself.Source: Annihilation of Caste, 1936
